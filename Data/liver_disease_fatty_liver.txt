NAFLD, NASH & FATTY LIVER DISEASE — Comprehensive Patient & Clinical Guide
Sources: Global Liver Institute, American Liver Foundation, EASL Guidelines 2024
Reference: https://globalliver.org/fatty-liver-resources/

=============================================================
SECTION 1: WHAT IS FATTY LIVER DISEASE?
=============================================================

Fatty liver disease occurs when excess fat builds up in the liver cells (hepatocytes). A liver with >5% of liver cells containing fat droplets is considered a "fatty liver" (steatosis). Fatty liver disease is now the most common liver condition worldwide, affecting an estimated 25-30% of the global population.

TERMINOLOGY UPDATE (2023 CONSENSUS)
The field adopted new terminology in 2023:
- NAFLD (Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease) → Now called MASLD (Metabolic-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease)
- NASH (Non-Alcoholic Steatohepatitis) → Now called MASH (Metabolic-Associated Steatohepatitis)
- The new names better reflect the metabolic root cause
- Old terms (NAFLD, NASH) are still widely used in clinical practice

TYPES OF FATTY LIVER
1. Metabolic-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD / formerly NAFLD):
   - Fat accumulation WITHOUT significant alcohol use
   - Most common form; strongly linked to metabolic syndrome

2. Metabolic-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH / formerly NASH):
   - Fat + liver cell inflammation + liver cell damage (ballooning)
   - Can progress to fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer
   - Approximately 20-25% of NAFLD patients develop NASH

3. Alcoholic Steatosis:
   - Fat accumulation DUE TO alcohol consumption (separate category)

4. ALD-MASLD (Dual etiology):
   - Both metabolic AND alcohol causes present

SECTION 2: RISK FACTORS & CAUSES
=============================================================

METABOLIC RISK FACTORS (Primary drivers)
- Obesity: BMI >30 increases risk 3-5 fold; visceral/abdominal obesity most important
- Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: Insulin resistance drives fat accumulation in liver
- High blood triglycerides (Hypertriglyceridemia)
- Low HDL cholesterol
- High blood pressure (Hypertension)
- Metabolic Syndrome: Having 3 or more of the above factors greatly increases risk

OTHER RISK FACTORS
- Age: More common over 50; however increasing in children and adolescents
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Hypothyroidism
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Rapid weight loss (paradoxically can worsen inflammation)
- Certain medications: Corticosteroids, tamoxifen, amiodarone, methotrexate, valproic acid
- Genetics: PNPLA3 gene variant (I148M) significantly increases NAFLD risk and severity

CHILDREN & ADOLESCENTS
NAFLD/MASLD is increasingly common in obese children:
- Affects 7-10% of all children; up to 34-38% of obese children
- Pediatric NAFLD has a different pattern: periportal fibrosis predominates
- Can progress to cirrhosis in young adults if untreated

=============================================================
SECTION 3: SYMPTOMS & CLINICAL PRESENTATION
=============================================================

EARLY STAGES (Simple Steatosis / MASLD)
- Usually NO symptoms (asymptomatic)
- May have mild fatigue or vague right upper quadrant discomfort
- Often discovered incidentally during routine blood tests or ultrasound

ADVANCED STAGES (MASH / Fibrosis / Cirrhosis)
- Fatigue (most common complaint)
- Right upper quadrant discomfort or dull ache
- Enlarged liver (hepatomegaly) on physical exam
- Elevated liver enzymes on blood tests (ALT typically > AST)
- As cirrhosis develops: Jaundice, ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy

KEY POINT: Most patients with NAFLD feel completely normal even with advanced disease.
Regular blood tests and imaging are essential for early detection.

=============================================================
SECTION 4: DIAGNOSIS
=============================================================

BLOOD TESTS
- ALT (Alanine aminotransferase): Mildly elevated; typically 1-4x upper limit of normal
- AST: Usually lower than ALT (ALT > AST pattern, unlike alcoholic disease)
- GGT: May be elevated, especially if there is metabolic syndrome
- Fasting glucose and HbA1c: Assess for diabetes
- Fasting lipid panel: Check triglycerides and HDL
- Complete metabolic panel
- Note: Up to 79% of NAFLD/NASH patients have NORMAL liver enzymes

NON-INVASIVE FIBROSIS SCORES
FIB-4 Index (Fibrosis-4):
Formula: FIB-4 = (Age × AST) / (Platelets × √ALT)
- FIB-4 <1.30: Low risk of advanced fibrosis (F0-F1)
- FIB-4 1.30-2.67: Indeterminate — needs further testing
- FIB-4 >2.67: High risk of advanced fibrosis (F3-F4)
Recommended as first-line screening by AASLD and EASL

NAFLD Fibrosis Score (NFS):
Uses: Age, BMI, impaired fasting glucose, AST/ALT ratio, platelets, albumin
- Low score (<-1.455): Rules out advanced fibrosis
- High score (>0.676): Predicts advanced fibrosis

ELF Test (Enhanced Liver Fibrosis):
- Blood test measuring hyaluronic acid, PIIINP, TIMP-1
- More accurate than FIB-4; used in specialist settings

IMAGING
Ultrasound Abdomen:
- First-line imaging; shows echogenic (bright/hyperechoic) liver
- Sensitivity ~85% for detecting steatosis >33%; misses mild steatosis
- Cannot stage fibrosis reliably

Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP):
- Measures liver fat using FibroScan device
- Scores: S0 (<5%), S1 (5-33%), S2 (33-66%), S3 (>66%) fat
- More sensitive than ultrasound for fat quantification

Transient Elastography (FibroScan):
- Measures liver stiffness for fibrosis staging
- Widely available; operator-dependent

MRI-Proton Density Fat Fraction (MRI-PDFF):
- Most accurate non-invasive method for quantifying liver fat
- Gold standard for clinical trials

LIVER BIOPSY
- Gold standard for definitive diagnosis and staging of NASH
- Required when non-invasive tests are indeterminate
- Histological features of NASH: Steatosis + lobular inflammation + hepatocyte ballooning ± fibrosis
- NAS (NAFLD Activity Score): 0-8; Score ≥5 = definitive NASH
- METAVIR Fibrosis Stage: F0-F4

SECTION 5: TREATMENT & MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

LIFESTYLE MODIFICATION (First-line for all patients)
Weight Loss:
- Most effective treatment for NAFLD/NASH
- 5-7% weight loss: Improves liver fat and inflammation
- 7-10% weight loss: Improves fibrosis in most patients
- >10% weight loss: Can reverse NASH in most patients
- Weight loss should be gradual (0.5-1kg/week); very rapid weight loss can worsen NASH

Exercise:
- Both aerobic exercise and resistance training improve NAFLD
- Target: 150-300 minutes moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week
- Reduces liver fat even without significant weight change

Diet:
Mediterranean Diet (most evidence):
- Rich in olive oil, vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, fish
- Low in red meat, processed foods, sugary drinks
- Reduces liver fat and inflammation
- Practical recommendations:
  - Eliminate sugary beverages (sodas, fruit juices, energy drinks)
  - Avoid high-fructose corn syrup products
  - Reduce refined carbohydrates (white bread, white rice, pastries)
  - Increase vegetables, fiber, whole grains
  - Choose healthy fats (olive oil, avocado, nuts) over saturated/trans fats
  - Limit red and processed meats
  - Coffee consumption (2-3 cups/day) may be protective for liver

Alcohol:
- Complete abstinence recommended for all NASH/MASLD patients
- Even moderate alcohol can worsen liver damage in NAFLD

PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT
FDA-Approved (2024):
Resmetirom (Rezdiffra):
- First FDA-approved drug specifically for NASH with liver fibrosis (F2-F3)
- Thyroid hormone receptor beta (THRβ) agonist
- Reduces liver fat and inflammation; improves fibrosis
- Dose: 80mg or 100mg orally once daily
- Significant milestone: First approved treatment specifically targeting MASH/NASH

Other treatments used in clinical practice (not specifically FDA-approved for NASH):
- Vitamin E (800 IU/day): Benefits non-diabetic NASH patients; avoid in diabetes, prostate cancer risk
- Pioglitazone (thiazolidinedione): Improves NASH histology, especially in diabetic patients
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide): Weight loss + liver fat reduction; promising data
- SGLT-2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): Improve liver fat and metabolic parameters
- Statins: Safe in NAFLD; protective against cardiovascular disease (not for liver fibrosis)

BARIATRIC SURGERY
- Most effective long-term treatment for obese NAFLD patients
- Roux-en-Y gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy both improve NASH and fibrosis
- Consider for BMI >35 with metabolic complications

LIVER TRANSPLANT
- For end-stage NASH cirrhosis or NASH-related HCC
- NASH is now the second most common reason for liver transplant in the US
- Recurrence of NAFLD in transplanted liver common unless metabolic syndrome controlled

=============================================================
SECTION 6: COMPLICATIONS OF UNTREATED NAFLD/NASH
=============================================================

- Liver fibrosis progression to cirrhosis: Occurs in 15-20% of NASH patients over 20 years
- Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC): Can occur even without cirrhosis in NASH (unique to this etiology)
- Cardiovascular disease: Leading cause of death in NAFLD patients (not liver-related)
- Type 2 Diabetes: NAFLD independently increases diabetes risk
- Chronic Kidney Disease: Associated with NAFLD, especially in diabetic patients
- Portal hypertension complications if cirrhosis develops

CARDIOVASCULAR RISK
NAFLD patients have 2-3x increased cardiovascular risk independent of traditional metabolic risk factors.
Annual cardiovascular risk assessment is essential for all NAFLD patients.

=============================================================
SECTION 7: NAFLD IN SPECIAL POPULATIONS
=============================================================

LEAN NAFLD
- Occurs in people with normal BMI (<25)
- More common in Asian populations (lower BMI threshold for diagnosis)
- Often driven by genetics (PNPLA3 variant), insulin resistance despite normal weight
- Can be more aggressive than obese NAFLD

NAFLD IN TYPE 2 DIABETES
- Prevalence of NAFLD in T2DM: 55-70%
- Faster fibrosis progression
- Higher risk of HCC
- GLP-1 agonists and SGLT-2 inhibitors particularly beneficial

NAFLD IN CHILDREN
- Management: Lifestyle modification (diet and exercise) is cornerstone
- No approved medications for pediatric NASH
- Vitamin E may be considered
- Monitor for comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia)

=============================================================
SECTION 8: MONITORING & FOLLOW-UP
=============================================================

For all NAFLD/MASLD patients:
- Annual liver function tests (ALT, AST, GGT)
- Annual FIB-4 calculation to monitor fibrosis risk
- Annual metabolic panel, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile
- Blood pressure monitoring
- FibroScan or liver biopsy if FIB-4 indeterminate or high risk
- Ultrasound + AFP every 6 months if cirrhosis present (HCC surveillance)
- Cardiovascular risk assessment annually

=============================================================
SECTION 9: KEY MESSAGES FOR PATIENTS
=============================================================

1. Fatty liver disease is largely REVERSIBLE in early stages with lifestyle changes
2. Weight loss of just 7-10% can dramatically improve liver health
3. Exercise helps even without weight loss
4. Mediterranean diet is the best evidence-based diet for liver health
5. Avoid alcohol completely — even small amounts can worsen the condition
6. Fatty liver usually has NO SYMPTOMS until advanced — don't wait for symptoms
7. Regular monitoring is essential even if you feel fine
8. Control diabetes, blood pressure, and cholesterol to protect your liver
9. Coffee may be protective — moderate coffee consumption is acceptable
10. Herbal supplements are NOT proven and many are hepatotoxic — inform your doctor
