TITLE: NHS UK — Liver Disease Guidelines, Warning Signs & Treatment
SOURCE: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/liver-disease/
AUTHORITY: National Health Service UK (Evidence Level 1A)
CLINICAL DOMAIN: Clinical Triage, Red Flags, Primary Care Management

NHS CLINICAL TRIAGE:
Liver disease can be silent in early stages. Many people only discover they have liver disease during routine blood tests.

EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS (RED FLAGS):
Seek immediate emergency medical attention (A&E / 999) if experiencing:
- Yellowing of the whites of your eyes or skin (jaundice).
- Vomiting blood or passing black, tarry, smelly stools (melena from bleeding varices).
- Swelling in your tummy area (ascites) that develops rapidly.
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, slurred speech, or personality changes (hepatic encephalopathy).
- Severe abdominal pain in the right upper quadrant.

TREATMENT AND REGIMEN:
- Stopping alcohol completely.
- Weight loss: Losing 10% of body weight can reduce liver fat and reverse early inflammation.
- Regular exercise: 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise weekly.
- Healthy diet: High in vegetables, whole grains, and healthy fats, avoiding sugary drinks and refined carbs.
