LATER-STAGE LIVER DISEASE: SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS
Source: Cleveland Clinic Medical Education
Category: Clinical Signs & Symptoms

=============================================================
OVERVIEW
=============================================================
Later-stage (advanced) liver disease refers to conditions such as
decompensated cirrhosis, end-stage liver disease (ESLD), and acute
liver failure. At this stage, the liver can no longer perform its
vital functions adequately. Recognizing these signs early is critical
for timely intervention.

=============================================================
8 KEY SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF LATER-STAGE LIVER DISEASE
=============================================================

1. JAUNDICE
   - Yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes (sclera)
   - Caused by accumulation of bilirubin (a yellow pigment) in the blood
   - Clinically visible when total bilirubin > 2-3 mg/dL
   - Associated: dark urine, pale stools, pruritus
   - Indicates: hepatitis, cirrhosis, bile duct obstruction, hemolysis
   - Action: Requires prompt medical evaluation — LFTs, imaging, biopsy

2. DARK-COLORED URINE
   - Tea-colored, cola-colored, or amber urine
   - Caused by excess conjugated bilirubin spilling into the urine
   - Classic sign of hepatitis A and E, cholestasis, acute liver injury
   - Also called bilirubinuria
   - Combined triad: dark urine + pale stool + jaundice = cholestasis
   - Action: Urgent LFTs, urinalysis, liver ultrasound

3. LIGHT-COLORED (PALE) STOOLS
   - Clay-colored, white, or grey stools (acholic stools)
   - Occurs when bile cannot reach the intestines
   - Bile normally gives stool its brown color via urobilinogen
   - Indicates: bile duct obstruction, PBC (primary biliary cholangitis),
     PSC (primary sclerosing cholangitis), pancreatic cancer, cirrhosis
   - Action: ERCP, MRCP, abdominal CT, liver function panel

4. DIGESTIVE DIFFICULTIES (ESPECIALLY WITH FATS)
   - Bloating, nausea, diarrhea after fatty meals
   - Fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies (A, D, E, K)
   - Steatorrhea: oily, foul-smelling, floating stools
   - Caused by reduced bile production or impaired bile flow
   - Can lead to significant malnutrition and weight loss
   - Management: low-fat diet, fat-soluble vitamin supplementation,
     pancreatic enzymes if needed, hepatology dietitian referral

5. WEIGHT LOSS AND MUSCLE LOSS (SARCOPENIA)
   - Present in up to 70% of patients with advanced cirrhosis
   - Causes: impaired protein synthesis, malabsorption, anorexia,
     hypermetabolism, hormonal imbalances
   - Sarcopenia independently worsens prognosis and increases mortality
   - Management: high-protein diet (1.2-1.5g/kg/day), branched-chain
     amino acids (BCAA), small frequent meals, avoid overnight fasting,
     light resistance exercise as tolerated
   - Never fast for prolonged periods with liver disease

6. MUSTY-SMELLING BREATH (FETOR HEPATICUS)
   - Sweet, musty, or "mousy" odor to the breath
   - Caused by: dimethyl sulfide, mercaptans, trimethylamine
   - These volatile compounds accumulate in blood due to liver failure
     and are exhaled through the lungs
   - Strongly associated with hepatic encephalopathy
   - Indicates: significant hepatic decompensation
   - Action: urgent hepatology evaluation, ammonia level testing

7. MILD BRAIN IMPAIRMENT (HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY - HE)
   - Spectrum of neuropsychiatric dysfunction caused by liver failure
   - Ammonia and other gut-derived toxins bypass liver detoxification
   - Grades:
       Grade 1: Mild confusion, altered sleep, mood changes
       Grade 2: Lethargy, disorientation, asterixis (hand flap)
       Grade 3: Somnolence, gross disorientation
       Grade 4: Coma
   - Key signs: asterixis (flapping hand tremor), altered sleep-wake cycle,
     forgetfulness, personality changes
   - Treatment: lactulose (2-3 soft stools/day), rifaximin 550mg BD,
     dietary protein optimization
   - Precipitants to avoid: infection, GI bleeding, constipation,
     dehydration, sedatives, electrolyte imbalance

8. PRURITUS (ITCHY SKIN WITHOUT VISIBLE RASH)
   - Intense, widespread itching without an obvious skin rash
   - Most distressing at night, on palms and soles
   - Caused by: lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), bile salts, endogenous opioids
     accumulating in skin due to cholestasis
   - Common in: PBC, PSC, intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, biliary obstruction
   - Treatment ladder:
       1st line: Cholestyramine 4g before meals
       2nd line: Rifampicin 150-300mg/day
       3rd line: Naltrexone 50mg/day or naloxone infusion
       4th line: Sertraline, UDCA
       Rescue: Plasmapheresis, molecular adsorbent recirculating system (MARS)
   - Soothing: cool water, loose cotton clothes, avoid hot showers

=============================================================
WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE
=============================================================
Call emergency services immediately if experiencing:
- Vomiting blood or black tarry stools (variceal bleeding)
- Sudden severe confusion or loss of consciousness
- Severe abdominal pain
- Jaundice that appears suddenly with high fever
- Yellow skin with inability to stay awake
